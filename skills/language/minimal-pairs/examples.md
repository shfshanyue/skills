# Minimal Pairs Worked Examples

Use these when you need a style sample for prompts or grading.

## L1 wrong answer

Prompt:

> Round 1 (L1 — Identify).  
> Pair: **light** /laɪt/ vs **right** /raɪt/  
> Target sentence: "Turn ___ at the corner to find the bakery."  
> Which word fits, and which phoneme is the giveaway?

Learner:

> light, because of /l/

Grading:

> **Score**: 1 / 5  
> **Correct answer**: `right /raɪt/` — "Turn right at the corner."  
> **Why this contrast is tricky**:  
> - /l/ touches the alveolar ridge; English /r/ curls or bunches back without contact.  
> - Mandarin learners often collapse English /r/ toward /l/.  
> **Mouth mechanics**:  
> - /l/: tongue tip presses behind the upper teeth.  
> - /r/: tongue tip floats backward, lips slightly rounded.  
> **Practice trio** (say these aloud 3× each):  
> - light — right — "Turn right at the right light."  
> **Now try again**:  
> - New prompt: pair **lock** /lɒk/ vs **rock** /rɒk/. "Don't trip over that ___ on the trail." Which word, and why?

## L2 vowel contrast

Prompt:

> Round (L2 — Disambiguate).  
> Pair: **sheep** /ʃiːp/ vs **ship** /ʃɪp/  
> "The ___ is on the wave." Which word fits, and what phoneme cue tells you?

A wrong answer should explain the semantic mismatch and the /iː/ vs /ɪ/ mouth cue; a correct answer gets the same 6 sections with a one-line praise replacing **Now try again**.
