# English Collocations Worked Examples

Use these when you need a grading or scenario style sample.

## Wrong answer

Round start:

> Scenario: Your team has been sitting on a half-finished search feature for three sprints. The PM wants it out this week.  
> Target collocation: **ship a feature**  
> Write one sentence using it.

Learner:

> We need to publish this search feature this week.

Grading:

> **Score**: 2 / 5  
> **Natural version**: "We need to ship this search feature this week."  
> **Why your collocation is off**:  
> - `publish` collocates with articles, papers, books, and blog posts. Software features get `ship`, `release`, or `roll out`.  
> **Synonym contrast**:  
> - `ship a feature` — common in engineering teams; get it into users' hands.  
> - `release a feature` — slightly more formal, often tied to a versioned release.  
> - `roll out a feature` — implies gradual or staged rollout.  
> **Counter-examples**:  
> - "We shipped a new research paper last quarter." — wrong; papers are `published`.  
> **Now try again**:  
> - Scenario: Your infra team finally finished the new rate limiter and it goes live tomorrow morning. Write one sentence using **ship a feature**.

## Correct answer + next round

After a correct answer, replace **Now try again** with one-line praise, add `---`, then append `## Next round` with Level, Pack, Scenario, Target collocation(s), and Task.

## L3 constraint

Pick the tense/voice/modal first, then write a scenario whose timeline supports it.

Example:

> Scenario: You finally paid off the credit card you opened in college; the balance hit zero this morning and you still feel relieved.  
> Target collocation: **pay off a debt**  
> Constraint: **present perfect**  
> Write one sentence.
